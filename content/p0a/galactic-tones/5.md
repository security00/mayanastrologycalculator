# Number 5 in the Tzolk'in (Dreamspell: "Overtone" Tone)

Number 5 is the fifth day of every thirteen-day run in the Tzolk'in. It is the first number written with a bar, because in bar-and-dot notation one bar equals five. Its head variant wears the *tun* sign as a headdress, which Morley called one of the most constant head numerals. The Classic Maya word was *ho'*. Dreamspell calls it the "Overtone" tone, a modern label. [1][2][3]

## How number 5 moves through the 260 days

The Tzolk'in pairs a running number from 1 to 13 with a running list of 20 day signs. Both advance by one each day, so a given pairing, such as 5 Chikchan, returns only after 260 days. [4][5] Number 5 therefore comes round every 13 days, each time with a different day sign: 20 times in each 260-day cycle. It is always the fifth day of a thirteen-day run. Spanish chroniclers called that run a *trecena*, "a group of thirteen", and Dreamspell calls it a "wavespell". [6] The day before a 5 is always a 4, and the day after is always a 6.

## The bar: how 5 is written

Bar-and-dot numbers use only two value marks: the dot (1) and the bar (5). Every number from 1 to 19 is a combination of the two. [2] The 18th-century Yucatec description reprinted by Morley puts it simply: "one line meant five years, two lines meant ten years" (Morley 1915: 91). [2] So 5 is a single bar, and every number from 5 to 13 in the Tzolk'in contains at least one bar.

On monuments the bar was often decorated. Morley illustrated many fives with internal patterns and stressed that "all these decorations… in no way affect the numerical value" (Morley 1915: 88). [2] If you are looking at a carved date, count the bars and dots and ignore the ornament.

## The head for 5

The head numeral for 5 carries a headdress shaped like the normal form of the *tun* sign, the glyph for the 360-day period. Morley found it so consistent that he called it "one of the most constant of all the head numerals". The same headdress marks the head for 15, which adds the fleshless jaw of 10 (Morley 1915: 99, 101). [2]

## Number 5 in early calendar records

Two of the early 260-day dates gathered by Stuart, Hurst, Beltrán and Saturno have a 5:

- **Jaina, Panel 3** (Campeche): a day sign read by its first publishers as **5 Imix**. Stuart and colleagues note that "the identification of the day sign remains difficult". By its style it dates to about 100 BCE, although the stone was later reused. [8]
- **La Mojarra, Stela 1** (Veracruz): **5 Deer (Manik') 15 Pop** with the Long Count 8.5.16.9.7, which they correlate to 12 July 156 CE. The text is in Isthmian (Epi-Olmec) script, not Maya, but it uses the same 260-day count. [8]

Mesoamerican peoples shared the 13 × 20 structure of the calendar, though the names of the days differed from language to language. The paper gives Nahuatl *Mazatl*, Zapotec *China* and K'iche' *Keej*, all meaning "Deer". [8]

## In living practice

We have not found a published source that gives the number 5 on its own a fixed meaning in highland daykeeping. The Dreamspell "Overtone" keywords (radiance, empower, command) are modern.

## Dreamspell: the "Overtone" tone (a modern system)

*This section describes the modern Dreamspell system (José & Lloydine Argüelles, from 1987; Dreamspell kit 1990), not the traditional Maya count.* [9]

In Dreamspell, tone 5 is called **"Overtone"**. The Foundation for the Law of Time lists its *essence* as radiance, its *power* as empower and its *action* as command. [3] These are Dreamspell teachings. They are not translations of a Maya word, and we have not found them in Classic, colonial or ethnographic sources.

Dreamspell also counts days differently. It fixes 26 July 1987 as Kin 34 and does not count 29 February. [10][9] So a Dreamspell tone 5 day is usually not a traditional number 5 day. For example, 14 Oct 2026 is 5 Chikchan in the traditional count but **Kin 49, "Red Planetary Moon"** in Dreamspell. ## Questions about number 5

**Is "galactic tone" a Maya term?**  
No. "Galactic tone", and "Overtone" as the name for 5, come from the modern Dreamspell system (from 1987). [9][11] Maya sources speak of the days' numbers. The English word *trecena* comes from Spanish chroniclers. [6]

**Why does another calculator give me a different number?**  
Most likely it uses Dreamspell, which anchors its count to 26 July 1987 and skips 29 February. The traditional count runs without a break and matches the dates published by Guatemala's Ministry of Culture. [10][12] A few calculators also use the 584285 or 584286 correlation, which moves results by two or three days. [13]

**Is 5 a good or bad number?**  
We have not found a traditional source that ranks the thirteen numbers, and we do not rank them.

**Why is 5 written as a bar?**  
Maya numerals combine dots (1) and bars (5). Five dots are replaced by a bar, just as five units become a bar in many tally systems. [2]

**Does the bar change the meaning of numbers 5–13?**  
No source we know of says so. The bar is a counting mark. Meanings sometimes given to "higher" numbers are modern.
