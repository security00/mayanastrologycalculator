# Number 9 in the Tzolk'in (Dreamspell: "Solar" Tone)

Number 9 is the ninth day of every thirteen-day run in the Tzolk'in. It is written as one bar and four dots. Its head variant, with dots on the cheek and sometimes a beard, is the most common head numeral after zero, because most Classic Long Count dates begin with 9 bak'tuns. The Classic Maya word is tentatively *balun*. K'iche' *b'elejeb'* appears in the name B'elejeb' Kej, "Nine Deer". Dreamspell calls 9 the "Solar" tone. [1][2][3][4]

## How number 9 moves through the 260 days

The Tzolk'in pairs a running number from 1 to 13 with a running list of 20 day signs. Both advance by one each day, so a given pairing, such as 9 Muluk, returns only after 260 days. [5][6] Number 9 therefore comes round every 13 days, each time with a different day sign: 20 times in each 260-day cycle. It is always the ninth day of a thirteen-day run. Spanish chroniclers called that run a *trecena*, "a group of thirteen", and Dreamspell calls it a "wavespell". [7] The day before a 9 is always an 8, and the day after is always a 10.

## How scribes wrote number 9

Nine is a bar and four dots: "if four dots above the line, nine" (Morley 1915: 91). [1] The four dots stay together as a group; Morley knew only one text where the dots of a "four" are separated (Morley 1915: 88, note 59). [1]

The head numeral for 9 "has for its essential characteristic the dots on the lower cheek or around the mouth". Sometimes it also has a beard, but the dots are the constant feature and reappear in the head for 19 (Morley 1915: 100). [1]

## Why the 9 head is so common

Morley explained why the head for 9 shows up so often. Most monumental dates fall in the ninth bak'tun (roughly 435–830 CE). So the opening Long Count of a typical Classic inscription, the Initial Series, starts with a 9: "9 is the coefficient attached to the cycle glyph in almost all Initial Series" (Morley 1915: 100). [1] This 9 is the bak'tun number in the Long Count, not the Tzolk'in day number, but it is the same numeral sign.

A separate cycle of nine also appears in the inscriptions. Kettunen and Helmke describe the Supplementary Series, which includes "a cycle of 9 days (glyph G)", probably tied to a ritual on the recorded date. [9] That nine-day cycle has nothing to do with the Tzolk'in's 1–13. We mention it because the two are often mixed up online.

## A 9 Imix text: the first cacao tree

In his 2025 note on the day Imix, David Stuart points to a text on a jade vessel from Burial 116 at Tikal. It refers to the sprouting of a primordial cacao tree on the day **9 Imix**. He calls it "probably a symbolic connection to the same idea of earthly abundance and sustenance" that he associates with Imix. [10] This is a scholar's reading of one mythological text. It is not a statement about what 9 or Imix means for people born on such days.

## Nine in K'iche' names

The K'iche' chronicles give us the word *b'elejeb'*:

- **B'elejeb' Kej** ("Nine Deer"). Christenson calls him "the first occurrence in the text of a historical figure whose name is derived from a day on the Highland Maya calendar", and adds that "it is likely the day on which he was born" (Christenson 2007: 246). [3]
- **B'elejeb' Tz'i'** ("Nine Dog"), described as "a day on the traditional 260 day calendar" (Christenson 2007: 276). [3]

## In living practice

We have not found a published source that gives the number 9 on its own a fixed meaning in highland daykeeping. The Dreamspell "Solar" keywords (intention, pulse, realize) are modern.

## Dreamspell: the "Solar" tone (a modern system)

*This section describes the modern Dreamspell system (José & Lloydine Argüelles, from 1987; Dreamspell kit 1990), not the traditional Maya count.* [11]

In Dreamspell, tone 9 is called **"Solar"**. The Foundation for the Law of Time lists its *essence* as intention, its *power* as pulse and its *action* as realize. [4] These are Dreamspell teachings. They are not translations of a Maya word, and we have not found them in Classic, colonial or ethnographic sources.

Dreamspell also counts days differently. It fixes 26 July 1987 as Kin 34 and does not count 29 February. [12][11] So a Dreamspell tone 9 day is usually not a traditional number 9 day. For example, 18 Oct 2026 is 9 Muluk in the traditional count but **Kin 53, "Red Magnetic Skywalker"** in Dreamspell. ## Questions about number 9

**Is "galactic tone" a Maya term?**  
No. "Galactic tone", and "Solar" as the name for 9, come from the modern Dreamspell system (from 1987). [11][13] Maya sources speak of the days' numbers. The English word *trecena* comes from Spanish chroniclers. [7]

**Why does another calculator give me a different number?**  
Most likely it uses Dreamspell, which anchors its count to 26 July 1987 and skips 29 February. The traditional count runs without a break and matches the dates published by Guatemala's Ministry of Culture. [12][14] A few calculators also use the 584285 or 584286 correlation, which moves results by two or three days. [15]

**Is 9 a good or bad number?**  
We have not found a traditional source that ranks the thirteen numbers, and we do not rank them.

**Is the 9 in "9 bak'tuns" the same as a Tzolk'in 9?**  
It is the same numeral but a different count. Bak'tun 9 is a position in the Long Count. A Tzolk'in 9 is the ninth day of a thirteen-day run. [1]

**Is the nine-day cycle in Maya inscriptions related to number 9?**  
No. The nine-day cycle (glyph G of the Supplementary Series) is a separate count. It is not the Tzolk'in number 9. [9]
