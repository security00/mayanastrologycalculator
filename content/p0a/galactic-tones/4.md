# Number 4 in the Tzolk'in (Dreamspell: "Self-Existing" Tone)

Number 4 is the fourth day of every thirteen-day run in the Tzolk'in. It is written with four dots, or in carved texts with a head with a large square-irised eye and a curling fang. The Classic Maya word was *chan* or *kan*. Four is the number of the Long Count's mythical creation date, 4 Ajaw 8 Kumk'u, and of 21 December 2012, which was 4 Ajaw 3 K'ank'in. Dreamspell calls it the "Self-Existing" tone. [1][2][3][4]

## How number 4 moves through the 260 days

The Tzolk'in pairs a running number from 1 to 13 with a running list of 20 day signs. Both advance by one each day, so a given pairing, such as 4 K'an, returns only after 260 days. [3][5] Number 4 therefore comes round every 13 days, each time with a different day sign: 20 times in each 260-day cycle. It is always the fourth day of a thirteen-day run. Spanish chroniclers called that run a *trecena*, "a group of thirteen", and Dreamspell calls it a "wavespell". [6] The day before a 4 is always a 3, and the day after is always a 5.

In the trecena that opened on **1 Imix, 10 October 2026**, number 4 falls on **13 Oct 2026**, the day **4 K'an** (Haab' 17 Yax). [7] The next eight days carrying number 4:

## How scribes wrote number 4

Four is four dots. In the inscriptions the four dots of 4, 9, 14 and 19 sit together as a group. Morley knew of only one text where they appear separated by an ornament: the west side of Stela E at Quiriguá, where the number 14 is written that way (Morley 1915: 88, note 59). [2]

The head for 4 has one of the most recognisable faces among the head numerals: a large, prominent eye with a square iris ("square irid"), a snag-like front tooth, and a curling fang at the back of the mouth (Morley 1915: 98). [2] Add the fleshless lower jaw of the head for 10 and you get 14. Morley knew that combination from a single text, Stela F at Quiriguá, where the jaw replaces the lower part of the face, fang included (Morley 1915: 101). [2]

## 4 Ajaw: the creation date and 2012

The Long Count counts days from a mythical starting point, written **13.0.0.0.0 4 Ajaw 8 Kumk'u**. The Smithsonian's *Living Maya Time* dates it to 11 August 3114 BCE and notes that it appears in hieroglyphs on Stela C at Quiriguá. [3] That is the GMT correlation (584283). Many epigraphers use the slightly adjusted 584285, which places the same day on 13 August 3114 BCE. [8][9]

When the count of thirteen bak'tuns came round again, on 21 December 2012, the Calendar Round date was **4 Ajaw 3 K'ank'in**. [3] By our calculation that day is exactly 1,872,000 days, or 13 × 144,000, after the creation date. The Tzolk'in position matches because 1,872,000 is a multiple of 260.

## K'atun 4 Ajaw

Colonial Yucatec chronicles named each 7,200-day k'atun after the day it ended on, and every k'atun ends on an Ajaw day (Morley 1915: 79–80). [5] In the GMT 584283 count, the k'atun that closed on 21 December 2012 began on 5 April 1993. It ended on 4 Ajaw, so in that naming system 1993–2012 was a *K'atun 4 Ajaw* (our calculation). The k'atun before it, which ended on 5 April 1993, was a K'atun 6 Ajaw. The numbers drop by two each time, exactly as Morley's rule predicts (Morley 1915: 80–81). [5]

## In living practice

We have not found a published source that gives the number 4 on its own a fixed meaning in highland daykeeping. Four directions and four Year Bearers matter in Maya thought, but those are sets of four *things*, not meanings of the Tzolk'in number 4. We do not merge the two.

## Dreamspell: the "Self-Existing" tone (a modern system)

*This section describes the modern Dreamspell system (José & Lloydine Argüelles, from 1987; Dreamspell kit 1990), not the traditional Maya count.* [10]

In Dreamspell, tone 4 is called **"Self-Existing"**. The Foundation for the Law of Time lists its *essence* as form, its *power* as define and its *action* as measure. [4] These are Dreamspell teachings. They are not translations of a Maya word, and we have not found them in Classic, colonial or ethnographic sources.

Dreamspell also counts days differently. It fixes 26 July 1987 as Kin 34 and does not count 29 February. [11][10] So a Dreamspell tone 4 day is usually not a traditional number 4 day. For example, 13 Oct 2026 is 4 K'an in the traditional count but **Kin 48, "Yellow Solar Star"** in Dreamspell. Our reports show the Dreamspell name as a labelled alias next to the traditional number. ## Questions about number 4

**Is "galactic tone" a Maya term?**  
No. "Galactic tone", and "Self-Existing" as the name for 4, come from the modern Dreamspell system (from 1987). [10][12] Maya sources speak of the days' numbers. The English word *trecena* comes from Spanish chroniclers. [6]

**Why does another calculator give me a different number?**  
Most likely it uses Dreamspell, which anchors its count to 26 July 1987 and skips 29 February. The traditional count runs without a break and matches the dates published by Guatemala's Ministry of Culture. [11][13] A few calculators also use the 584285 or 584286 correlation, which moves results by two or three days. [9]

**Is 4 a good or bad number?**  
We have not found a traditional source that ranks the thirteen numbers, and we do not rank them.

**Why do some sources put the Maya creation date on 13 August 3114 BCE?**  
They use the 584285 correlation, which is two days later than the GMT 584283 used here. The day name, 4 Ajaw, is the same in both. [9][8]

**Our sample pair report uses 21 December 2012. Why?**  
It is a well-documented 4 Ajaw date, so readers can check it against the Smithsonian. [3] Person B in the [sample pair report](/sample-compatibility-report) was born on that date.

**Is 4 a number of stability?**  
Words such as *form*, *define* and *measure* belong to the Dreamspell "Self-Existing" tone. [4] We have not found them in traditional sources.
