# Number 10 in the Tzolk'in (Dreamspell: "Planetary" Tone)

Number 10 is the tenth day of every thirteen-day run in the Tzolk'in. It is written as two bars. Its head variant is a skull, and the skull's fleshless lower jaw was added to the heads for 3 to 9 to write 13 to 19, so it works like a "ten" digit. The Classic Maya word was *lajun*. K'iche' *laju(j)* survives in the place name Xe' Laju, "Below Ten [No'j]". Dreamspell calls 10 the "Planetary" tone. [1][2][3][4]

## How number 10 moves through the 260 days

The Tzolk'in pairs a running number from 1 to 13 with a running list of 20 day signs. Both advance by one each day, so a given pairing, such as 10 Ok, returns only after 260 days. [5][6] Number 10 therefore comes round every 13 days, each time with a different day sign: 20 times in each 260-day cycle. It is always the tenth day of a thirteen-day run. Spanish chroniclers called that run a *trecena*, "a group of thirteen", and Dreamspell calls it a "wavespell". [7] The day before a 10 is always a 9, and the day after is always an 11.

## How scribes wrote number 10

Ten is two bars: "two lines meant ten years" (Morley 1915: 91). [2] With two bars and no dots, 10 is one of the easier numbers to read on a weathered monument. Numbers from 11 to 13 add one, two or three dots above the bars.

## The skull head: a "ten" you can add

Morley called the head for 10 "extremely important". It is "clearly the fleshless skull, having the truncated nose and fleshless jaws". Its key feature, the fleshless lower jaw, "stands for the numerical value 10". Scribes attached that jaw to the heads for 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 to write 13 through 19 (Morley 1915: 100). [2] Most head numerals are shown in profile. Morley knew one front-facing 10, on Zoomorph B at Quiriguá, where the fleshless jaw runs across the whole lower face (Morley 1915: 100). [2]

The system has exceptions. The heads for 11 and 12 do *not* carry the skull's jaw, so they were not built as 10 + 1 and 10 + 2 (Morley 1915: 100). [2] For 13, scribes had two choices: a head of its own, or the head for 3 plus the jaw of 10 (Morley 1915: 101). [2] See [Number 11](/galactic-tones/11), [Number 12](/galactic-tones/12) and [Number 13](/galactic-tones/13).

## Ten in a K'iche' place name

The K'iche' chronicles name a settlement in the southern Quetzaltenango basin **Xe' Laju**, "Below Ten". Christenson explains that it is a shortened form of Xe' Laju[j No'j], "Below Ten No'j", where Ten No'j is "a day on the traditional highland Maya calendar". He notes that its exact location is unknown (Christenson 2007: 260). [3] The name gives us the K'iche' word *laju(j)* for ten. It also shows that calendar days could name places as well as people.

## In living practice

We have not found a published source that gives the number 10 on its own a fixed meaning in highland daykeeping. The skull head is a writing convention, and we do not read it as a "death" meaning for people born on a 10. The Dreamspell "Planetary" keywords (manifestation, perfect, produce) are modern.

## Dreamspell: the "Planetary" tone (a modern system)

*This section describes the modern Dreamspell system (José & Lloydine Argüelles, from 1987; Dreamspell kit 1990), not the traditional Maya count.* [9]

In Dreamspell, tone 10 is called **"Planetary"**. The Foundation for the Law of Time lists its *essence* as manifestation, its *power* as perfect and its *action* as produce. [4] These are Dreamspell teachings. They are not translations of a Maya word, and we have not found them in Classic, colonial or ethnographic sources.

Dreamspell also counts days differently. It fixes 26 July 1987 as Kin 34 and does not count 29 February. [10][9] So a Dreamspell tone 10 day is usually not a traditional number 10 day. For example, 19 Oct 2026 is 10 Ok in the traditional count but **Kin 54, "White Lunar Wizard"** in Dreamspell. Our reports show the Dreamspell name as a labelled alias next to the traditional number. ## Questions about number 10

**Is "galactic tone" a Maya term?**  
No. "Galactic tone", and "Planetary" as the name for 10, come from the modern Dreamspell system (from 1987). [9][11] Maya sources speak of the days' numbers. The English word *trecena* comes from Spanish chroniclers. [7]

**Why does another calculator give me a different number?**  
Most likely it uses Dreamspell, which anchors its count to 26 July 1987 and skips 29 February. The traditional count runs without a break and matches the dates published by Guatemala's Ministry of Culture. [10][12] A few calculators also use the 584285 or 584286 correlation, which moves results by two or three days. [13]

**Is 10 a good or bad number?**  
We have not found a traditional source that ranks the thirteen numbers, and we do not rank them.

**Does the skull head mean that 10 is a number of death?**  
No source we know of says so. The skull is the conventional head numeral for 10 in inscriptions. [2] It is a way of writing the number, not a reading for people.

**Why don't 11 and 12 use the skull jaw?**  
Morley observed that their heads lack the jaw, so they were not formed as 10 + 1 or 10 + 2. Later research has identified at least the 11 head as a distinct deity. [2][14]
