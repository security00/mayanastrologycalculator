# Number 2 in the Tzolk'in (Dreamspell: "Lunar" Tone)

Number 2 is the second day of every thirteen-day run in the Tzolk'in. Scribes wrote it as two dots. Its portrait-head form is so rare that Morley knew only two examples. The Classic Maya word was probably *cha'*, and Allen Christenson's Popol Vuh commentary glosses K'iche' *kaib'* as "simply the number two". In Dreamspell it is the "Lunar" tone, a modern label. ## How number 2 moves through the 260 days

The Tzolk'in pairs a running number from 1 to 13 with a running list of 20 day signs. Both advance by one each day, so a given pairing, such as 2 Ik', returns only after 260 days. [5][6] Number 2 therefore comes round every 13 days, each time with a different day sign: 20 times in each 260-day cycle. It is always the second day of a thirteen-day run. Spanish chroniclers called that run a *trecena*, "a group of thirteen", and Dreamspell calls it a "wavespell". [7] The day before a 2 is always a 1, and the day after is always a 3.

In the trecena that opened on **1 Imix, 10 October 2026**, number 2 falls on **11 Oct 2026**, the day **2 Ik'** (Haab' 15 Yax). [8] The next eight days carrying number 2:

## How scribes wrote number 2

Two is two dots. [2] An 18th-century Yucatec writer, quoted by Brinton and reprinted by Morley, listed the bar-and-dot numbers from 1 to 13. Two dots appear twice in that list, once on their own and once above two bars: "two dots for two years… a dot above two lines, eleven; if two dots, twelve" (Morley 1915: 91). [2] Like 1, 6, 7, 11, 12, 16 and 17, the number 2 left gaps in a square glyph block. Monument carvers often filled the gap with decorative elements that have no numerical value, and on weathered stones those fillers can be mistaken for a third dot (Morley 1915: 88–91). [2]

The head variant for 2 is one of the least understood. Morley found only two examples, on Lintel 2 at Piedras Negras and on the tablet of the Temple of the Initial Series at Holactun. He accepted an oval at the top of the head as its distinguishing mark while "admitting at the same time that the evidence is insufficient" (Morley 1915: 98). [2]

## Number 2 in K'iche' and in the Popol Vuh

In Christenson's annotated Popol Vuh, a K'iche' lord appears as K'o Kaib', "Lord Two". Christenson explains that "Kaib' is simply the number two". He gives several possible reasons for the name: a large posterity, an important division of the lineage, or the second generation of lords (Christenson 2007: 236). [3] This is a personal name, not a day of the calendar. We cite it only for the K'iche' word.

## Number 2 and the current k'atun

The Maya Long Count groups days into larger periods. A k'atun is 7,200 days, about 19.7 years. A k'atun always ends on a day named Ajaw, and colonial Yucatec chronicles named each k'atun after the full name of its ending day, as in "Katun 2 Ahau" (Morley 1915: 79–80). [6] Because 7,200 leaves a remainder of 11 when divided by 13, each k'atun's ending number is two lower than the one before. That is why the sequence runs 13, 11, 9, 7, 5, 3, 1, 12, 10… (Morley 1915: 80–81). [6]

You can apply this to the present. The Long Count reached 13.0.0.0.0 on 21 December 2012, a day 4 Ajaw. [5] With the GMT 584283 count used on this site, the next k'atun ending falls on **7 September 2032**, and that day is **2 Ajaw**. In the colonial naming system, the k'atun we are living in is therefore a *K'atun 2 Ajaw*. (This is our calculation from the published rule. With the 584285 correlation the Gregorian date moves two days later, but the day name stays the same.) [8]

## In living practice

We have not found a published source that gives the number 2 on its own a fixed meaning in highland daykeeping. Ideas of "duality" or "challenge" attached to it online come from the Dreamspell "Lunar" tone or from modern interpretation, and we label them that way.

## Dreamspell: the "Lunar" tone (a modern system)

*This section describes the modern Dreamspell system (José & Lloydine Argüelles, from 1987; Dreamspell kit 1990), not the traditional Maya count.* [9]

In Dreamspell, tone 2 is called **"Lunar"**. The Foundation for the Law of Time lists its *essence* as challenge, its *power* as polarize and its *action* as stabilize. [4] These are Dreamspell teachings. They are not translations of a Maya word, and we have not found them in Classic, colonial or ethnographic sources.

Dreamspell also counts days differently. It fixes 26 July 1987 as Kin 34 and does not count 29 February. [10][9] So a Dreamspell tone 2 day is usually not a traditional number 2 day. For example, 11 Oct 2026 is 2 Ik' in the traditional count but **Kin 46, "White Resonant World-Bridger"** in Dreamspell. ## Questions about number 2

**Is "galactic tone" a Maya term?**  
No. "Galactic tone", and "Lunar" as the name for 2, come from the modern Dreamspell system (from 1987). [9][11] Maya sources speak of the days' numbers. The English word *trecena* comes from Spanish chroniclers. [7]

**Why does another calculator give me a different number?**  
Most likely it uses Dreamspell, which anchors its count to 26 July 1987 and skips 29 February. The traditional count runs without a break and matches the dates published by Guatemala's Ministry of Culture. [10][12] A few calculators also use the 584285 or 584286 correlation, which moves results by two or three days. [13]

**Is 2 a good or bad number?**  
We have not found a traditional source that ranks the thirteen numbers, and we do not rank them.

**Does number 2 mean duality in Maya tradition?**  
We have not found a traditional source that says so. The Dreamspell "Lunar" tone has the keywords *challenge*, *polarize* and *stabilize*, and those are modern. [4]

**What is a K'atun 2 Ajaw?**  
It is a roughly 20-year period named after its ending day. The one now running ends on 2 Ajaw, which falls on 7 September 2032 in the GMT 584283 count (our calculation). [6]
