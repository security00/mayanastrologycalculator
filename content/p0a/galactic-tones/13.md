# Number 13 in the Tzolk'in (Dreamspell: "Cosmic" Tone)

Number 13 is the highest number in the Tzolk'in. After 13 the count returns to 1, so 13 Ajaw is always followed by 1 Imix. It is written as two bars and three dots. Its head variant has two forms, and David Stuart links one to the Water Serpent. Thirteen also frames the Long Count era of 13 bak'tuns, which ended on 21 December 2012. The Classic Maya word was *uxlajun* or *oxlajun*. Dreamspell calls 13 the "Cosmic" tone. [1][2][3][4][5][6]

## How number 13 moves through the 260 days

The Tzolk'in pairs a running number from 1 to 13 with a running list of 20 day signs. Both advance by one each day, so a given pairing, such as 13 Ben, returns only after 260 days. [4][1] Number 13 therefore comes round every 13 days, each time with a different day sign: 20 times in each 260-day cycle. It is always the thirteenth day of a thirteen-day run. Spanish chroniclers called that run a *trecena*, "a group of thirteen", and Dreamspell calls it a "wavespell". [7] The day before a 13 is always a 12, and the day after is always a 1.

In the trecena that opened on **1 Imix, 10 October 2026**, number 13 falls on **22 Oct 2026**, the day **13 Ben** (Haab' 6 Sak'). [8] The next eight days carrying number 13:

## How scribes wrote number 13

Thirteen is two bars and three dots. The 18th-century Yucatec description reprinted by Morley ends its list exactly here: "a dot above two lines, eleven; if two dots, twelve; if three dots, thirteen" (Morley 1915: 91). [2]

As a head, 13 had two forms (Morley 1915: 101). [2]

- **A composite**: the head for 3, with its headband, plus the fleshless lower jaw of the head for 10. Morley knew only two examples, on the Temple of the Cross at Palenque and the Hieroglyphic Stairway at Copan.
- **A head of its own**, "by far the more frequent". It is a grotesque face with a long pendulous nose topped by a curl, a large bulging eye, and a curl or fang at the back of the mouth.

David Stuart's 2025 note on Imix adds an identification. The Water Serpent, the being behind the Imix day sign, "could also appear as the head for the number 'thirteen'" (citing Robertson 1990). He suggests the link may come from a mythic period ending on **13 Ajaw 13 Kumk'u**, a pre-era k'atun station recorded on Lacanjá-Tzeltal Panel 1. [3]

## The top of the count: 13 Ajaw → 1 Imix

Morley's rule: the sequence of numbers "repeats itself again and again without interruption, 1 following immediately 13" (Morley 1915: 43). [1] Because Ajaw is the last of the twenty names and 13 is the last number, the day before every 1 Imix is 13 Ajaw (Morley 1915: 43). [1] In practice: **9 October 2026 was 13 Ajaw**, and 10 October 2026 opened a new round with 1 Imix (GMT 584283). [8]

## Thirteen in the Long Count and beyond

- **13 bak'tuns.** The Smithsonian describes the 13-bak'tun cycle as 1,872,000 days, about 5,125 years, ending on 21 December 2012. [4] That ending was a 4 Ajaw day, not a 13: the number 13 here counts bak'tuns, not Tzolk'in days.
- **K'atun 13 Ajaw.** In the colonial naming of k'atuns by their ending day, the next "K'atun 13 Ajaw" ends on **25 May 2052** in the GMT 584283 count (our calculation from Morley's rule). [1]
- **Thirteen score days.** The Popol Vuh describes K'iche' lords fasting and sacrificing in periods of "thirteen score days", that is, 13 × 20 days. Christenson notes that the "score" is implied in the original wording (Christenson 2007: 268–269, note 826). [9]
- **Early records.** La Mojarra Stela 1 in Veracruz records **13 Snake (Chikchan) 3 Kayab** with a Long Count that Stuart and colleagues correlate to 20 May 143 CE. It is in Isthmian script, not Maya. [10]

## In living practice

Contemporary Guatemalan sources link the thirteen numbers to the body. In 2025 the Ministry of Culture wrote that the twenty nawales are related to the twenty digits of the human body, and that they are "linked with the 13 levels of energy, and these represent the 13 joints of the body" (our translation of "se vinculan con los 13 niveles de energía y estas representan a las 13 articulaciones del cuerpo"). [11] We report this as a contemporary view published by the Ministry. We do not treat it as an ancient inscription or apply it to individual birth dates.

## Dreamspell: the "Cosmic" tone (a modern system)

*This section describes the modern Dreamspell system (José & Lloydine Argüelles, from 1987; Dreamspell kit 1990), not the traditional Maya count.* [12]

In Dreamspell, tone 13 is called **"Cosmic"**. The Foundation for the Law of Time lists its *essence* as presence, its *power* as endure and its *action* as transcend. [6] These are Dreamspell teachings. They are not translations of a Maya word, and we have not found them in Classic, colonial or ethnographic sources.

Dreamspell also counts days differently. It fixes 26 July 1987 as Kin 34 and does not count 29 February. [13][12] So a Dreamspell tone 13 day is usually not a traditional number 13 day. For example, 22 Oct 2026 is 13 Ben in the traditional count but **Kin 57, "Red Overtone Earth"** in Dreamspell. Our reports show the Dreamspell name as a labelled alias next to the traditional number. [Is "Cosmic" a Maya term? How the two counts differ →](/dreamspell-vs-traditional-maya-calendar)

## Questions about number 13

**Is "galactic tone" a Maya term?**  
No. "Galactic tone", and "Cosmic" as the name for 13, come from the modern Dreamspell system (from 1987). [12][14] Maya sources speak of the days' numbers. The English word *trecena* comes from Spanish chroniclers. [7]

**Why does another calculator give me a different number?**  
Most likely it uses Dreamspell, which anchors its count to 26 July 1987 and skips 29 February. The traditional count runs without a break and matches the dates published by Guatemala's Ministry of Culture. [13][11] A few calculators also use the 584285 or 584286 correlation, which moves results by two or three days. [15]

**Is 13 a good or bad number?**  
We have not found a traditional source that ranks the thirteen numbers, and we do not rank them.

**Is 13 the most powerful number?**  
We have not found a traditional source that ranks the numbers. Thirteen is the highest number, after which the count returns to 1. "Presence", "endure" and "transcend" are the Dreamspell keywords for "Cosmic". [1][6]

**Why did 2012 end a cycle of 13?**  
The Long Count era is counted as 13 bak'tuns (13 × 144,000 days), and 21 December 2012 completed that count. It was a 4 Ajaw day in the Tzolk'in. [4]

**Is Dreamspell's "13 Moon" calendar the same as the 13 numbers?**  
No. The 13 Moon calendar is a Dreamspell year of thirteen 28-day months plus a "Day Out of Time". Its months are named after the tones, but it is not a Maya calendar. [12]
