## Whose dates are these?

Neither is a real couple. Both dates are public reference points that anyone can check. The Smithsonian gives **1 January 2000** as 11 Ik' 10 K'ank'in and **21 December 2012**, the end of the 13th bak'tun, as 4 Ajaw 3 K'ank'in. [1] Our calculator converts each date the same way: Julian Day Number − 584,283 = days; number = ((days + 3) mod 13) + 1; day sign = (days + 19) mod 20. For 21 December 2012 that is 2,456,283 − 584,283 = 1,872,000 days. That gives number 4 and day sign index 19, Ajaw. [2] The report shows the Dreamspell names of the two numbers ("Spectral" for 11, "Self-Existing" for 4) and labels them as a modern Dreamspell layer on its methodology page.

## About the score

The percentage is a **modern synthesis** of five things: the distance between the two numbers, the distance between the two day signs, and the element, direction and color assigned to each sign. The report says plainly that "it is not a historical Maya matching rite". Changing the relationship lens changes the wording, not the number. Read it as a starting angle for conversation, not as a verdict. Our [compatibility page](/compatibility) describes it as "a modern interpretive feature, not a documented historical Maya matching formula".
