# Dreamspell vs the Traditional Maya Count: Why Your Sign Can Differ

If a Dreamspell or 13 Moon site gave you a different "kin" from the one on this site, neither calculator is broken. They follow two different calendars. Dreamspell is a modern system created by José and Lloydine Argüelles from 1987, with a kit published in 1990. It sets 26 July 1987 as Kin 34 and does not count 29 February. [1][2] The traditional count used here runs without a break and matches the dates published for the living highland calendar. [3][4]

## Why the results differ

There are two reasons: a different anchor, and a different rule for leap days.

**1. A different anchor.** The traditional count has no fixed starting day of its own. It is simply the next day in a sequence that has run for more than two thousand years. To convert a Gregorian date, you need a *correlation constant*: the Julian Day Number of the Long Count's starting point. The Goodman–Martínez–Thompson (GMT) value is 584,283. Floyd Lounsbury later proposed 584,285, two days later, and many epigraphers use that figure. [4][14] Mark Van Stone notes that the strongest evidence for the GMT over GMT+2 is that day-keepers in Momostenango, recorded by Dennis and Barbara Tedlock, "were still using the 260-day calendar, and it fits the GMT". [4]

Dreamspell does not use a correlation constant. In the words of the Foundation for the Law of Time's *Rinri* newsletter, "this count was reformulated precisely so that July 26, 1987, would be White Galactic Wizard". That is Kin 34. The same passage names 26 July 1993 as Yellow Magnetic Seed (Kin 144) and 26 July 2013 as Yellow Galactic Seed (Kin 164), and the newsletter adds that Kin 1 fell 33 days before the 1987 anchor, on 23 June 1987. [2] In the traditional count, 26 July 1987 was **6 Ajaw 8 Xul**. Dreamspell's Kin 34 occupies the slot that the traditional count calls 8 Ix (position 34 counting from 1 Imix). So the two systems were already 54 positions apart on the day Dreamspell was anchored.

**2. A different leap-day rule.** Dreamspell skips 29 February. On that day it records "0.0 Hunab Ku" and then continues the next day as if no time had passed. [1] The traditional count never pauses. The *Rinri* newsletter explains the choice by noting that in the Chilam Balam texts "leap year was never considered, much less counted in the Year-Bearer Count". [2] (The 365-day Haab' also has no leap day, but the 260-day count keeps counting every day.) [4]

The result is a gap that changes over time. Each 29 February, Dreamspell holds still while the traditional count moves on one day, so the difference drops by one every four years:

| Date | Traditional count | Dreamspell | Gap (positions) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 26 Jul 1987 | 6 Ajaw (position 240) | Kin 34 | 54 |
| 1 Jan 2000 | 11 Ik' (position 102) | Kin 153 | 51 |
| 21 Dec 2012 | 4 Ajaw (position 160) | Kin 207 | 47 |
| 10 Oct 2026 | 1 Imix (position 1) | Kin 45 | 44 |

*Positions count from 1 Imix = 1 to 13 Ajaw = 260. Dreamspell's Kin 1 "Red Magnetic Dragon" occupies the same slot as 1 Imix, so a kin number and a traditional position can be compared directly. Our calculation; method below.*

Because the gap is never zero, the two systems will almost never give the same day sign *and* number for the same birth date.

### If you were born on 29 February

In the traditional count your birth day has a sign and number like any other day. For 29 February 2000 it is 5 Imix. In Dreamspell your birth date has no galactic signature. It is "0.0 Hunab Ku". [1] Dreamspell practitioners have their own conventions for this, and we do not try to settle them here. Our reports always use the traditional result.

## Terms you'll meet on Dreamspell sites

These words belong to the Dreamspell system. A Lake Atitlán culture site notes that kin, solar seal, galactic tone and wavespell "do not come from any Maya language or community". [13] Each has a rough traditional counterpart:

| Dreamspell term | What it means there | Closest traditional idea |
|---|---|---|
| Kin (1–260) | Position in the 260-day "galactic spin" | A day in the Tzolk'in, named by number + day sign |
| Solar seal | One of the 20 named images (Red Dragon …) | One of the [20 day signs](/day-signs) |
| Galactic tone | One of the 13 named numbers (Magnetic …) | The [day number 1–13](/galactic-tones) |
| Galactic signature | Tone + color + seal, e.g. "Red Planetary Skywalker" | The day name, e.g. 11 Ik' |
| Wavespell | A 13-kin run | A *trecena* |
| 13 Moon calendar | 13 × 28 days + Day Out of Time | No traditional equivalent. The traditional solar year is the Haab' (18 × 20 + 5) [6] |

So when someone asks "What is my galactic signature?", the traditional answer is your day name: a number and a day sign, counted from your birth date without skipping days.

## Who uses the traditional count today

The 260-day count is not only an archaeological object. The Smithsonian's *Living Maya Time* describes highland Guatemalan daykeepers who "celebrate a new year ceremony called Wajxaqib', B'atz", when "new calendar Day Keepers are initiated". [6] Guatemala's Ministry of Culture published 18 January 2025 and the government portal 5 October 2025 as Waqxaqib' B'atz' dates. [3][15] With GMT 584283, both days come out as **8 Chuwen**, the Yucatec name for the day K'iche' calls B'atz'. [10] This public, checkable agreement is the main reason we use the GMT count. See [Number 8](/galactic-tones/8) for upcoming dates.

## Why this site uses the traditional count

- **It matches living practice.** As shown above, our dates match the 8 B'atz' dates published in Guatemala. [3][4]
- **It is transparent.** Our [methodology page](/methodology) publishes the full formula: subtract 584,283 from the Julian Day Number, then number = ((days + 3) mod 13) + 1 and day index = (days + 19) mod 20. [8]
- **It is stable.** We are not changing the count, so nobody's result changes. Reports already issued keep the same day sign and number. Only the labels are clearer.
- **We name the alternatives.** Some scholars and tools use 584285 or 584286, which move results by two or three days. [8][4]

We still show the Dreamspell name next to your number, clearly labelled, because many readers know it. We do not mix the two systems or present Dreamspell names as Maya terms. In the words of the AtitlanVida guide, "presenting one as the other does a disservice to both". [13]

## How the converter calculates

*For readers who want to check our numbers.*

**Traditional (GMT 584283)** [8]
1. JDN = Julian Day Number of the date (proleptic Gregorian).
2. days = JDN − 584283.
3. Number = ((days + 3) mod 13) + 1. Day sign index = (days + 19) mod 20, where 0 = Imix and 19 = Ajaw.
4. Haab' position = (days + 348) mod 365. Positions 0–359 are 18 months of 20 days starting at Pop, and 360–364 are Wayeb'.

Check: 1 January 2000 → JDN 2,451,545 → days 1,867,262 → **11 Ik' 10 K'ank'in**, Long Count 12.19.6.15.2, as given by the Smithsonian. [6]

**Dreamspell** [2][1]
1. If the date is 29 February, return "0.0 Hunab Ku".
2. d = days from 26 July 1987 to the date, not counting any 29 February in between (negative for earlier dates).
3. Kin = ((33 + d) mod 260) + 1. Tone = ((Kin − 1) mod 13) + 1. Seal = ((Kin − 1) mod 20) + 1, with 1 = Red Dragon … 20 = Yellow Sun.

Check: this reproduces every anchor quoted in the *Rinri* newsletter: Kin 1 on 23 June 1987, Kin 34 on 26 July 1987, Kin 55 and 56 on 16–17 August 1987, Kin 144 on 26 July 1993 and Kin 164 on 26 July 2013. [2]

## Questions

**Which one is right?**  
They answer different questions. The traditional count is the calendar kept by Maya daykeepers. Dreamspell is a modern calendar with its own rules. Each is internally consistent. Our reports use the traditional count.

**Can I use my Dreamspell kin to order a report here?**  
No. Reports are generated from your birth date using the traditional count. Enter your date, not your kin, and the report shows your Dreamspell name as a labelled alias.

**I was born on 29 February. What is my sign?**  
In the traditional count, you have a normal day name. For example, 29 February 2000 is 5 Imix. In Dreamspell the date is "0.0 Hunab Ku" and has no kin. [1]

**What is the difference between 584283, 584285 and 584286?**  
They are correlation constants: different estimates of how the Maya Long Count lines up with our calendar. 584283 (GMT) is used here. 584285 is Lounsbury's adjustment, two days later. 584286 is a further variant some tools offer. [4][8] The Dreamspell difference is much larger and is not a correlation question at all.

**Has this site changed my result?**  
No. The calculation has always been GMT 584283. What changed is the labelling: numbers come first and Dreamspell names are marked as Dreamspell.
