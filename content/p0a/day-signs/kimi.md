# Kimi (Kame): The Death Day, Sixth Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** Kimi is the sixth of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar; in K'iche' it is Kame. Its names in several Mayan languages come from roots meaning "to die", and its glyph shows the skull of the death god. In the K'iche' Popol Vuh, the lords of the underworld are named One Death and Seven Death after this day. Highland records also describe it as the day above all others for asking forgiveness. Dreamspell calls this slot "White World-Bridger". [1][3][4][6]

## Where Kimi sits in the 260-day count

Kimi follows Chikchan and precedes Manik'; 5 Chikchan is followed by 6 Kimi. [1] Like every day sign, it meets each number from 1 to 13 once in 260 days. [2] In the 365-day Haab', Kimi shares its possible positions (4, 9, 14 and 19 of a month) with Imix, Chuwen and Kib. [1]

## What the name means

This is one of the least disputed names in the calendar. Thompson wrote in 1950 that the Yucatec name "is beyond question from the same root as *cimil*, 'to die', 'death'". [4] Kettunen and Helmke list the Classic Maya verb *cham*, "to die", and give Cham? as a tentative Classic reading of the day. [1] In K'iche', Christenson's dictionary has *kämik*, "to die", and *kämikal*, "death", alongside the day name Kame. [5] The Aztec day in the same position was Miquiztli, "death". [4]

Thompson also recorded an older Chiapas and western Guatemalan name, Tox. The colonial bishop Núñez de la Vega described "13 Tox" as a horned demon. Thompson argued that the horns were a European idea of the devil, added after a century and a half of Christian teaching, and that Tox, like the Popol Vuh's death lords, was originally a god of the underworld. [4]

## The glyph

The day sign has two forms. The head form is the skull of the death god. The simpler symbolic form is a mark that Thompson called "the peculiar percentage sign", an attribute of the death gods in the codices. [4] The augural animal of the day in Yucatec lists is the *cuz* owl, "which in Maya belief was a portent of death". [4]

Thompson summed up: "The name and picture of the sixth day unquestionably refer to the death god." [4] He also noted a surprise: "strangely, the day is considered lucky in one respect or another by the Quiche, the Ixil, and the people of Santa Eulalia." [4] The highland records below show why.

## One Death and Seven Death in the Popol Vuh

In the K'iche' Popol Vuh, the lords of Xibalba, the underworld, are called One Death and Seven Death. The story introduces them when they hear the hero twins' father and uncle playing ball on the road to Xibalba. [6] Allen Christenson's translation notes that "Kame (Death) is a named day on the highland Maya calendar. The death lords thus both bear calendric dates as their names." [6]

These are names taken from the 260-day count, the same practice by which people were named after their birth day. [3] They do not make the day itself "evil" in any simple sense.

## Kame in highland tradition

The 260-day count is still kept in highland Guatemala by Ajq'ijab' (daykeepers). [2] The records of Kame are mixed:

- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina's calendar lists Kame as a "bad day". [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), describe Kame as "symbolic of the ultimate dissolution of all things, good and evil, in death", and as "the day above all others for forgiveness or pardon for all the evil deeds that one has committed". [3]
- **A good day for some.** The same summary notes that for some it is a good day, "for it signifies that one's evil deeds will be forgiven and that sickness will pass". [3]
- **The west.** K'iche' daykeepers interviewed by Estrada Peña assign Kame to the west, "the region where the sun 'dies' and descends into the underworld". [7]

These records describe ritual use in specific communities. They are not predictions for anyone born on Kimi, and a "death" day does not foretell death. We are not daykeepers. Our reports treat Kimi as modern reflective writing about endings, release and forgiveness.

## Traditional Kimi vs Dreamspell "White World-Bridger"

"White World-Bridger" is seal 6 in the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990. [10] Dreamspell gives it the keywords Death, Equalizes and Opportunity. [9] The name "World-Bridger" is Dreamspell's own; it is not a translation of a Maya word.

The two systems count differently. Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [10][11] 15 Oct 2026 is **6 Kimi** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 50, White Spectral Dog. The next Dreamspell "White World-Bridger" day, 11 Oct 2026 (Kin 46, White Resonant World-Bridger), is **2 Ik'** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

- **Prehispanic pattern: north.** The days were assigned to east, north, west and south in turn. Estrada Peña, citing López Austin, notes that this order would put Kame in the north. [7]
- **Contemporary K'iche' practice: west.** Today Kame belongs to the west, where the sun "dies". [7] Both placements are documented for different periods.
- **Color.** "White" follows the north–white pairing of colonial Yucatec texts; assigning it to one day is a modern reconstruction. [12] Dreamspell also colors this seal white. [9]
- **Element.** Some charts, including earlier versions of this site, give Kimi the element "earth". We found no traditional source for Greek-style elements on day signs; on this site it appears only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Are Kimi, Cimi and Kame the same day?**
Yes. Cimi is the colonial Yucatec spelling, Kimi the revised one, and Kame (or Keme) the K'iche' name. [1][3]

**Is Kimi a bad sign to be born on?**
Historical sources disagree, and none of them is a personal forecast. One 1854 list calls Kame a bad day; 20th-century K'iche' accounts also call it a day of forgiveness, and some consider it good. [3]

**Is "White World-Bridger" the same as Kimi?**
It fills the same slot in Dreamspell's list, but Dreamspell's count differs, so a "World-Bridger" birthday is usually not a Kimi day. [9][10]

**Who are One Death and Seven Death?**
The lords of the underworld in the Popol Vuh, named after the days 1 Kame and 7 Kame. [6]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [8][13]

**Is Kimi a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day that recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
