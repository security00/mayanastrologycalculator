# K'an (K'at): The Ripe Maize Day, Fourth Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** K'an is the fourth of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar. In K'iche' the same day is K'at. The Yucatec word *k'an* means "yellow" and "ripe", and the day has long been tied to ripe maize; David Stuart describes the glyph as "a maize tamale in its origin". The K'iche' name K'at means "net". The label "Seed" is the Dreamspell name ("Yellow Seed"), not a translation of the Maya day. [1][4][5][6]

## Where K'an sits in the 260-day count

K'an follows Ak'bal and precedes Chikchan, so 3 Ak'bal is followed by 4 K'an. [1] Over 260 days K'an meets every number from 1 to 13 once. [2] In the 365-day Haab' it can only fall on positions 2, 7, 12 or 17 of a month, which it shares with Muluk, Ix and Kawak. [1]

## What the name means

In Yucatec, *k'an* is an everyday word. Kettunen and Helmke gloss the Classic adjective K'AN as "yellow" and "ripe", and note it as a color term used, for example, to describe cacao drinks. [1] Thompson listed further senses in 1950: Yucatec *kan* also means "cord" and "any netted cord", and related words for "ripe" occur in Q'eqchi' and Ch'ol. [4]

The K'iche' name points the same way from a different angle. Christenson's dictionary glosses *k'at* as "net; cord; bag made of cords". [5] So the lowland "cord" sense and the highland "net" sense overlap, even though the two names are different words.

K'iche' daykeepers also play on the sound of the name. Christenson, citing Barbara Tedlock, notes that in divination "the meaning of the day C'at may be derived from c'atic (to burn), pa c'at (in nets), or c'asaj c'olic (to be in debt)", each valid in its context. [7] This is a good reminder that a day name in living practice is a cluster of associations, not a single keyword.

## The glyph: from tamale to maize grain

Thompson saw the K'an sign as grains of maize. Young maize plants grow out of K'an signs in the codices, and a maize plant growing from a K'an sign forms the headdress of the maize god and other deities. He added that K'an signs often accompany food offerings, so the word could stand for food in general, "our daily bread". [4] He pointed to the Kaua augury, which links K'an with *yumil ixim*, "the lord of the maize grain". [4]

David Stuart's recent work refines this. In discussing the day Ben, he describes K'an as "a maize tamale in its origin". [6] He also groups K'an with day signs that were abbreviated from a feature on a deity's forehead, the same visual shorthand that turned Imix into a waterlily. [11] In other words, the abstract cross-shaped sign is a shorthand for a food made of maize, linked to the young maize god.

## K'an in ancient texts

Thompson's 1950 count of Classic monument dates (excluding major period endings) found K'an among the most frequent days, with 35 occurrences, after Ajaw, Imix, Kaban and Lamat. [4] Thompson himself cautioned that monument dates were chosen for astronomical and ritual reasons, so frequency says little about whether a day was considered lucky.

## K'at in highland tradition

The 260-day count is still kept in highland Guatemala by Ajq'ijab' (daykeepers). [2] Published records of K'at differ from the lowland "ripe maize" picture:

- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina's calendar lists K'at as a "bad day", like the day before it. [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), describe K'at as "symbolic of evil in general"; 7 Aq'ab'al and 8 K'at were days to pray for protection against the envy of others. [3]
- **Rank among the Mam.** Christenson's dictionary calls K'at the "Secretary/Treasurer Mam", a role among the day lords. [5]
- **Personal names.** Colonial sources quoted by Weeks and colleagues report that K'iche' and Kaqchikel children took the name of their birth day; K'at is among the day names recorded as personal names. [3]

These records describe practice in particular places and times. They are not forecasts for anyone born on K'an. We are not daykeepers. Our reports use the image of ripening maize as modern reflective writing: what in your life is ready to be gathered?

## Traditional K'an vs Dreamspell "Yellow Seed"

"Yellow Seed" is seal 4 in the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990. [10] Dreamspell gives it the keywords Flowering, Targets and Awareness. [9] Many websites, including earlier versions of this one, label K'an "Seed" for this reason. The Maya day is about ripe maize and the color yellow, which is close, but "Seed" is Dreamspell's word.

The two systems also count differently. Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [10][12] 13 Oct 2026 is **4 K'an** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 48, Yellow Solar Star. The next Dreamspell "Yellow Seed" day, 29 Oct 2026 (Kin 64, Yellow Crystal Seed), is **7 Ajaw** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

- **Direction.** Prehispanic calendars assigned the days to east, north, west and south in turn, which places the fourth day in the **south**. [13] Contemporary K'iche' practice ties the south to Q'anil (Lamat) instead, not to K'at. [13]
- **Color.** The south is yellow in colonial Yucatec texts, and *k'an* itself means yellow. [1][11] Dreamspell also colors this seal yellow. [9]
- **Element.** Some charts, including earlier versions of this site, give K'an the element "fire". We found no traditional source assigning Greek-style elements to day signs. It appears on this site only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Are K'an and K'at the same day?**
Yes. K'an (older spelling Kan) is the Yucatec name and K'at the K'iche' name for the fourth day. [1][3]

**Is K'an the "Seed" sign?**
"Seed" is the Dreamspell label ("Yellow Seed"). The Maya word means "yellow, ripe", and the glyph is linked to maize. [1][4][9]

**What does K'an mean?**
In Yucatec, "yellow" and "ripe", and also "cord"; in K'iche', K'at means "net". [1][4][5]

**Is "Yellow Seed" the same as K'an?**
It fills the same slot in Dreamspell's list, but Dreamspell's count differs, so a "Yellow Seed" birthday is usually not a K'an day. [9][10]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [8][14]

**Is K'an a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day that recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
