# Kib (Ajmaq): The Sixteenth Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** Kib is the sixteenth of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar; in K'iche' it is Ajmaq. The Yucatec name means "wax", and Ajmaq is usually translated "sinner". In highland Guatemala it is a day of the ancestors and of penitence. Its oldest meaning is unclear, and the English label "Owl", common online, has no source we could find. Dreamspell calls this slot "Yellow Warrior". [1][3][5][6]

## Where Kib sits in the 260-day count

Kib follows Men and precedes Kaban; 2 Men is followed by 3 Kib. [1] In the 365-day Haab' Kib can only fall on positions 4, 9, 14 or 19 of a month, the same set as Imix, Kimi and Chuwen. [1]

## The names: wax, sinner, insect

Kib has several names, and they do not obviously agree. Thompson wrote in 1950 that "Cib means wax; Ah Mac is said to signify sinner", and that a third name, Chabin, survives in Hun Chabin, "1 Cib", the name of a pyramid near Comitán in Chiapas. [5]

- **Kib, "wax".** A colonial Yucatec creation story in the Book of Chilam Balam of Chumayel says that "on 6 Cib the first candle was made". Thompson pointed out that candles were a post-Columbian introduction, so this cannot be the day's original sense. [5]
- **Ajmaq, "sinner".** Christenson's K'iche' dictionary lists Ajmak as a day in the sacred calendar and *ajmak* as "one who is guilty; sinner". [6]
- **Ajmaq, an insect.** The ethnographer Leonhard Schultze Jena, working in Chichicastenango, recorded that "the real meaning of this word is associated with an insect, which the Indians call ahmak", which agrees with the fact that on this day people make offerings to the souls of their ancestors, who are imagined "in the form of this insect which is smaller than a fly". An aged informant told him that Ajmaq meant "flying insect". [5]

## Where does "Owl" come from?

Many websites, including earlier versions of this one, label Kib "Owl". We have not found a published source for it. The names above give wax, sinner and insect; the central Mexican day is the king vulture; and one colonial Yucatec list, the Kaua manuscript, connects the day with the deer god. [5] Until a source turns up, we do not use "Owl" as a meaning of Kib.

## The glyph

The Kib glyph has an abstract "symbolic" form and a rarer head form. Thompson thought the symbolic form was "almost surely a section of a univalve shell", and noted that a head variant on Yaxchilan Lintel 48 has the features of the jaguar god of the number 7. [5]

From these and the codex pages that pair Kib with offerings to insects (Madrid 103–106), Thompson built a long hypothesis linking the day to the Bakabs, sky-bearers and patrons of beekeeping, and to the souls of the dead returning as insects. He himself wrote: "The case is not proved." [5] We report it as a 1950 hypothesis, not a settled reading. Kettunen and Helmke leave the Classic reading of Kib blank. [1]

## Ajmaq in highland tradition

The 260-day count is still kept in highland Guatemala by Ajq'ijab' (daykeepers). [2] Ajmaq is tied to the dead and to moral reflection:

- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina's calendar calls Ajmaq "also a most excellent day", "especially consecrated to the spirits presiding over good health". [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), describe it as "a day without defined character but symbolic of the moral forces as embodied in penitential rituals". 7 Tz'ikin, 8 Ajmaq and 9 No'j "are days in which to pray for protection". [3]
- **Ancestors.** Thompson cites the anthropologist Sol Tax, who reported that at Chichicastenango the day is that of ancestral spirits. [5]

The "sinner" gloss is a dictionary meaning of the word. These records describe how communities used the day; they say nothing about the character of people born on it, and neither do we. We are not daykeepers. Our reports use Kib's themes of memory, forgiveness and ancestry as modern reflective writing.

## Traditional Kib vs Dreamspell "Yellow Warrior"

"Yellow Warrior" is seal 16 in the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990. [9] Dreamspell gives it the keywords Intelligence, Questions and Fearlessness. [8] "Warrior" is Dreamspell's own name; it does not translate Kib or Ajmaq.

The counts also differ: Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [9][10] 25 Oct 2026 is **3 Kib** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 60, Yellow Galactic Sun. The next Dreamspell "Yellow Warrior" day, 21 Oct 2026 (Kin 56, Yellow Self-Existing Warrior), is **12 Eb** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

- **Direction.** Prehispanic calendars assigned the days to east, north, west and south in turn, which places the sixteenth day in the **south**. [4] Contemporary K'iche' practice gives the south to Q'anil instead. [4]
- **Color.** The south is yellow in colonial Yucatec texts; assigning that color to one day is a modern reconstruction. [11] Dreamspell also colors this seal yellow. [8]
- **Element.** Some charts, including earlier versions of this site, give Kib the element "water". We found no traditional source for Greek-style elements on day signs; on this site it appears only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Are Kib, Cib and Ajmaq the same day?**
Yes. Kib (Cib in older spelling) is the Yucatec name and Ajmaq the K'iche' name for the sixteenth day. [1][3]

**Does Kib mean "owl"?**
Not in any source we found. The names mean "wax" (Yucatec) and "sinner" (K'iche'); the central Mexican day is the king vulture. [5][6]

**Does being born on Ajmaq mean someone is a "sinner"?**
No. That is a dictionary meaning of the word. Highland records describe it as a day for penitence and for the ancestors. [3][6]

**Is "Yellow Warrior" the same as Kib?**
It fills the same slot in Dreamspell's list, but Dreamspell's count differs, so a "Yellow Warrior" birthday is usually not a Kib day. [8][9]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [7][12]

**Is Kib a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day that recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
