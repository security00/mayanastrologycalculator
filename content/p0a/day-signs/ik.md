# Ik' (Iq'): The Wind Day, Second Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** Ik' is the second of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar. In K'iche' it is Iq'. It is one of the few day names whose meaning is not in doubt: the same T-shaped glyph writes the word *ik'*, "wind, air, breath", in Classic Maya inscriptions. In highland Guatemala Iq' is one of the four year bearers. The modern Dreamspell system calls this slot "White Wind". [1][3][5]

## Where Ik' sits in the 260-day count

Ik' follows Imix and precedes Ak'bal. Because the 13 numbers and 20 names advance together, the day after 1 Imix is 2 Ik', and Ik' meets every number once in 260 days. [1][2] In the Haab', the 365-day calendar that ran alongside, an Ik' day can only fall on positions 0, 5, 10 or 15 of a month. [1] Kettunen and Helmke list the same coefficients for Manik', Eb and Kaban. Those four are, by name, exactly the four days that serve as year bearers in the K'iche' calendar: Iq', Kej, E and No'j. [1][3]

## What the name means

Ik' is the clearest of the twenty names. In Classic Maya script the logogram IK' is read *ik'* and glossed "air, wind, breath" in Kettunen and Helmke's sign list, and the same sign is used as the day name. [1] The K'iche' word *iq'* also means "wind", and Iq' is the K'iche' day name. [5] J. Eric S. Thompson noted in 1950 that "almost all sources agree" on the meaning and that the Aztec day in the same position was Ehecatl, the wind god. [4]

Breath carries the idea of life. Thompson wrote that Ik' and its highland equivalent "mean not only wind but breath and by extension life itself". [4] Classic texts used the same sign in a metaphor for death. On Yaxchilan Lintel 27, the deaths of a king and his mother are written as the withering of "his/her white wind/breath" (*sak ik'il*). [1]

## The glyph

The day sign is a simple T shape inside the day cartouche. Thompson pointed out that the same T appears in the name glyph of Schellhas' "God B", a rain deity. [4] In the Madrid Codex the young maize god carries Ik' signs in a section about the growing maize crop, and other gods hold Ik' signs from which plants sprout. Thompson read this as germination, "of coming to life". [4] He concluded that the day belonged to a rain deity and to life and fruitfulness in general.

That interpretation is Thompson's. Later scholarship has not overturned the reading *ik'*, "wind/breath", which is the secure part. The theological identification with God B remains his mid-20th-century view.

## Iq' as a year bearer

In the K'iche' calendar each 365-day year is named after the day that begins it, and only four days can do so: No'j, Iq', Kej and E. [3] These are the year bearers. Christenson's K'iche' dictionary describes Iq' as the "fourth ranked Mam", the term for the year-bearer lords. [5]

The four days had a similar standing among the Ixil Maya of Chajul. In a note dated 30 August 1913, Robert Burkitt recorded: "Éh, Noh, Iq, and Txéh, are the lucky days. The other 16 indifferent. Everybody knows these days." [3]

## How Iq' is understood in highland tradition

The 260-day count is still kept in highland Guatemala by Ajq'ijab' (daykeepers). [2] Records of Iq' vary:

- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina's calendar lists Iq' as a "bad day", following Imox. [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), describe Iq' as a day associated with destructive forces embodied in stone idols, which are honored with offerings of incense, aguardiente, roses, pine needles and candles. [3]
- **Year bearer.** Chajul's "lucky days" note above, written in 1913, gives a different picture from the 1854 list. [3]

These sources describe what calendar specialists recorded in specific places and times. They are not predictions for people born on Ik'. We are not daykeepers. Our reports use the wind and breath imagery above as modern reflective writing, not traditional teaching.

## Traditional Ik' vs Dreamspell "White Wind"

Here the two systems use the same English word, which makes confusion more likely. "White Wind" is seal 2 in the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990. [9] Dreamspell gives it the keywords Spirit, Communicates and Breath. [8] These are modern Dreamspell meanings.

The day count is different. Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [9][10] So a "White Wind" day is usually not an Ik' day. 11 Oct 2026 is **2 Ik'** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 46, White Resonant World-Bridger. The next Dreamspell "White Wind" day, 27 Oct 2026 (Kin 62, White Planetary Wind), is **5 Etz'nab** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

- **Direction.** Prehispanic Mesoamerican calendars assigned the days to east, north, west and south in turn. That places Ik', the second day, in the **north**. [6] Contemporary K'iche' practice assigns only four nawales to the directions (Aq'ab'al, Imox, Kame, Q'anil), and Iq' is not one of them. [6]
- **Color.** "White" follows from the north–white pairing in colonial Yucatec texts; assigning it to a single day is a modern reconstruction. Dreamspell also colors this seal white. [8]
- **Element.** Some charts, including earlier versions of this site, give Ik' the element "air". That is an understandable modern gloss on "wind", but no traditional source we found assigns Greek-style elements to day signs. It is shown on this site only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Is Ik' the same as Iq'?**
Yes. Ik' is the Yucatec spelling and Iq' the K'iche' spelling of the same day. Older books write Ik. [1][3]

**Does Ik' really mean wind?**
Yes. The glyph writes the word *ik'*, "wind, air, breath", in Classic Maya texts, and the meaning is shared by Yucatec and K'iche'. [1][5]

**Is "White Wind" the same as Ik'?**
Same name, different calendar. Dreamspell runs its own count, so a Dreamspell "White Wind" birthday is usually not an Ik' day. [8][9]

**What is a year bearer?**
In the K'iche' calendar, a day that can begin and name a 365-day year. Iq' is one of four, with Kej, E and No'j. [3]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [7][11]

**Is Ik' a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day, not a month or star sign. It recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
