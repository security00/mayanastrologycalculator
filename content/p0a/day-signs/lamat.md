# Lamat (Q'anil): The Star Day, Eighth Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** Lamat is the eighth of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar; in K'iche' it is Q'anil. The Lamat glyph is the Maya sign for the planet Venus, a "star". In the highlands, Q'anil is read as "yellowness", the color of ripe maize, and 8 Q'anil is a day of thanksgiving for food and land. The common English label "Rabbit" is borrowed from the Aztec day Tochtli. Dreamspell calls this slot "Yellow Star". [1][4][5][6]

## Where Lamat sits in the 260-day count

Lamat follows Manik' and precedes Muluk; 7 Manik' is followed by 8 Lamat. [1] Over 260 days it meets every number once. [2] In the 365-day Haab' it can only fall on positions 1, 6, 11 or 16 of a month, which it shares with Ak'bal, Ben and Etz'nab. [1]

## The glyph: a Venus sign

J. Eric S. Thompson stated it directly in 1950: "The glyph for Lamat is the sign for the planet Venus." In the head variants, he wrote, "the celestial dragon with Venus symbols on his body is clearly recognizable." [4] The name itself is harder. Thompson noted that "the day names Lamat and Lambat have no obvious connection with Venus". [4] Kettunen and Helmke give Lamaht? as a possible Classic reading, with a question mark. [1]

David Stuart has pointed out that Lamat is one of several day signs abbreviated to a deity's eye, the same scribal shorthand that reduced other days to a single feature. [7] The "star" form most people recognize is that abbreviation.

## Where does "Rabbit" come from?

From Central Mexico. The Aztec day in the same position was Tochtli, "rabbit". [4] Thompson found a thin link: rabbits were a symbol of drunkenness on the Mexican plateau, and the Maya Venus god was described in the colonial Book of Chilam Balam of Chumayel as walking abroad "like one drunk". He called this "a tenuous connection". [4]

Many websites, including earlier versions of this one, label Lamat "Rabbit". On this site we treat it as a cross-cultural alias. The Maya glyph shows a star, and the highland name refers to the color of ripe maize.

## Lamat in ancient texts

Lamat is one of the more common days on Classic monuments. Thompson counted 37 occurrences in his sample of non-period-ending dates, fourth after Ajaw, Imix and Kaban. [4] Part of that comes from repetition: Palenque's inscriptions return again and again to the date 5 Lamat 1 Mol, a habit Thompson flagged as "over-weighting" his count. [4]

## Q'anil: ripe maize and thanksgiving

The highland name has a different story. Thompson suggested that Kanil and Khanil came from K'anal, "star", in several Chiapas languages, and that once this origin was forgotten, diviners heard in it a word for ripeness. [4] That was his reconstruction. K'iche' speakers today hear *q'an*, "yellow", in the name, and Estrada Peña reports that this is why Q'anil is the nawal of the south, whose color is yellow. [5]

The documented highland meanings are consistent and positive:

- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina calls Q'anil a "good day, sacred to the spirits of agriculture", when people ask for all that sustains human life. [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), describe it as "symbolic of the regeneration of the earth, of rebirth after death, and seen in the growth of corn. It is the day of the milpa." [3]
- **8 Q'anil.** Christenson's Popol Vuh notes quote Bunzel: "it is obligatory for all people to give thanks for their food and their land on the day 8 qanil." [6]
- **"The face of the earth becomes yellow."** Barbara Tedlock reported this phrase as a Momostenango daykeeper's mnemonic for Q'anil, meaning harvest. [6]

These are records of community practice. They are not forecasts for anyone born on Lamat. We are not daykeepers. Our reports draw on the image of a ripening field and a morning star as modern reflective writing.

## Traditional Lamat vs Dreamspell "Yellow Star"

"Yellow Star" is seal 8 in the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990. [10] Dreamspell gives it the keywords Elegance, Beautifies and Art. [9] The "star" name happens to match the Maya glyph, but the keywords are Dreamspell's own.

The counts differ. Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [10][11] 17 Oct 2026 is **8 Lamat** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 52, Yellow Cosmic Human. The next Dreamspell "Yellow Star" day, 13 Oct 2026 (Kin 48, Yellow Solar Star), is **4 K'an** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

Lamat is the one day sign where the old pattern and today's K'iche' practice agree:

- **Direction: south.** Prehispanic calendars assigned the days to east, north, west and south in turn, which places the eighth day in the south. Contemporary K'iche' daykeepers also place Q'anil in the south. Estrada Peña notes that of the four days he examined, it is the only one in the same place under both systems. [5]
- **Color: yellow.** The south is yellow in colonial Yucatec texts, and K'iche' ceremonies set yellow candles on the south side. [5][12] Dreamspell also colors this seal yellow. [9]
- **Element.** Some charts, including earlier versions of this site, give Lamat the element "water". We found no traditional source for Greek-style elements on day signs; on this site it appears only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Are Lamat and Q'anil the same day?**
Yes. Lamat is the Yucatec name and Q'anil (older Kanil) the K'iche' name for the eighth day. [1][3]

**Is Lamat the rabbit sign?**
Only by analogy. "Rabbit" translates the Aztec day Tochtli. The Maya glyph is a Venus sign. [4]

**What does Lamat mean?**
The Yucatec word is unexplained; the glyph is the sign for Venus. The K'iche' name Q'anil means "yellowness", the yellow of ripe maize. [4][6]

**Is "Yellow Star" the same as Lamat?**
It fills the same slot in Dreamspell's list, but Dreamspell's count differs, so a "Yellow Star" birthday is usually not a Lamat day. [9][10]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [8][13]

**Is Lamat a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day that recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
