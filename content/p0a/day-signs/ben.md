# Ben (Aj): The Young Maize Day, Thirteenth Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** Ben is the thirteenth of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar; in K'iche' it is Aj. Across Mesoamerica the matching day is usually "Reed", but K'iche' *aj* also means a young ear of maize. David Stuart's 2025 study traces the Ben glyph to a maize cob with two husks, worn on the head of the ancient Maize God. "Reed" is a later reading, not the day's original meaning among the early Maya. Dreamspell calls this slot "Red Skywalker". [1][3][5]

## Where Ben sits in the 260-day count

Ben follows Eb and precedes Ix; 12 Eb is followed by 13 Ben, and the next day starts the numbers over at 1 Ix. [1] In the 365-day Haab' Ben can only fall on positions 1, 6, 11 or 16 of a month, the same set as Ak'bal, Lamat and Etz'nab. [1]

## The names: Ben and Aj

The lowland name has no clear meaning. Stuart notes that Ben or Be'en was the day name in Yucatec, Tzeltal, Chuj and Q'anjob'al, with a possible Ch'ol cognate Bin, and that "these similar forms have no obvious etymology". In modern Chuj, Be'e'n is reported as the name of a deity. [5] Thompson found the Yucatec root "somewhat sterile", supplying mainly words about going or departing. [6]

The highland name is clearer and has two senses. Aj is "universally translated as 'reed' (caña)", Stuart writes, but it also means *elote*, a fresh maize cob. The colonial friar Ximénez glossed *ah* both as "la caña" and "la mazorca tierna" (young ear of corn). [5] Thompson reported the same pair for the K'iche', "with an apparent preference for reed or cane", and noted that the Ixil, Jakaltek and Santa Eulalia lists also tie the day to cane or maize. [6] Stuart suggests the two meanings may come from two originally separate Proto-Mayan words. [5]

## The glyph: from maize cob to abstract sign

In the Classic period the Ben glyph was uniform: a horizontal line, vertical lines below and two small loops above. Stuart shows that it is an abstraction of an earlier trefoil, "almost flower-like", seen on Stela 114 at Calakmul and in paintings at Uaxactun and Río Azul. [5]

He then traces that trefoil back to Middle Formative imagery, where it marks a leafy maize cob on the head of the Maize God, a motif that also became the jewel of the so-called Jester God on royal headbands. A unique head variant of Ben shows a young face with the "IL" mark often found on the Maize God. [5] Thompson had noticed a similar personified Ben on Piedras Negras Lintel 3 and concluded in 1950 that "Ben represents the growing maize plant, whereas kan is the grain of ripe maize". [6] Stuart says Thompson was "generally right". [5]

## Why "Reed"?

The Aztec day in this position was Acatl, "reed". Stuart argues that the Nahua "Reed" sign still carries "vestiges of the old trefoil maize design", inherited through Zapotec and Ñuiñe writing. He asks whether "Reed" elsewhere in Mesoamerica arose as "an imperfect borrowing from Mayan *aj*, giving preference to one possible translation over another", and concludes that "'Reed' was not the original meaning of the thirteenth day among the early Maya". [5]

Many websites, including earlier versions of this one, label Ben "Reed". On this site we keep it as a cross-cultural alias and describe the Maya day by its maize imagery.

## Aj in highland tradition

The 260-day count is still kept in highland Guatemala by Ajq'ijab' (daykeepers), whose very title contains the word for "day" (*q'ij*). [2] Records of Aj are favorable:

- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina's calendar calls Aj "again a good day", consecrated to the gods of agriculture and to those over flocks and domestic animals. [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), describe Aj as "symbolic of destiny as embodied in the nawal; the day of one's destiny". [3]
- **Children and fields.** Thompson noted that the K'iche' regarded the day as very favorable for children, and that in Jakaltenango it was good for prayers for the cornfields. [6]
- **A personal name.** The name Wuqub' Aj, "Seven Reed/Cane", appears in the Popol Vuh; Christenson notes that it is "a day in the traditional 260 day calendar". [7]

These are records of community practice, not forecasts for people born on Ben. We are not daykeepers. Our reports use the image of young maize, growth that is not yet ripe, as modern reflective writing.

## Traditional Ben vs Dreamspell "Red Skywalker"

"Red Skywalker" is seal 13 in the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990. [10] Dreamspell gives it the keywords Space, Explores and Wakefulness. [9] "Skywalker" is Dreamspell's own name; it does not translate Ben or Aj.

The counts differ too. Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [10][11] 22 Oct 2026 is **13 Ben** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 57, Red Overtone Earth. The next Dreamspell "Red Skywalker" day, 18 Oct 2026 (Kin 53, Red Magnetic Skywalker), is **9 Muluk** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

- **Direction.** Prehispanic calendars assigned the days to east, north, west and south in turn, which places the thirteenth day in the **east**. [4] Contemporary K'iche' practice gives the east to Aq'ab'al instead. [4]
- **Color.** "Red" follows the east–red pairing of colonial Yucatec texts; assigning it to one day is a modern reconstruction. [12] Dreamspell also colors this seal red. [9]
- **Element.** Some charts, including earlier versions of this site, give Ben the element "fire". We found no traditional source for Greek-style elements on day signs; on this site it appears only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Are Ben and Aj the same day?**
Yes. Ben (Been) is the Yucatec name and Aj the K'iche' name for the thirteenth day. [1][3]

**Is Ben the reed sign?**
Partly. K'iche' *aj* means both "reed" and "young maize", and Stuart argues the glyph began as a maize cob; "Reed" became the main reading outside the Maya area. [5]

**What does Ben mean?**
The Yucatec word has no clear meaning. The glyph represents young maize on the Maize God's head. [5][6]

**Is "Red Skywalker" the same as Ben?**
It fills the same slot in Dreamspell's list, but Dreamspell's count differs, so a "Red Skywalker" birthday is usually not a Ben day. [9][10]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [8][13]

**Is Ben a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day that recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
