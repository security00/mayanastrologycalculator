# Manik' (Kej): The Deer Day, Seventh Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** Manik' is the seventh of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar. In K'iche' it is Kej, the word for "deer", and across Mesoamerica the matching day is "Deer". Yet the usual Maya glyph is a hand. David Stuart suggests the hand began as a deer's antler that scribes later reinterpreted. The oldest securely dated Maya calendar record, from San Bartolo (300–200 BCE), is 7 Manik' written with a deer's head. Dreamspell calls this slot "Blue Hand". [1][3][5][6]

## Where Manik' sits in the 260-day count

Manik' follows Kimi and precedes Lamat; 6 Kimi is followed by 7 Manik'. [1] Over 260 days Manik' meets every number once. [2] In the 365-day Haab' it can only fall on positions 0, 5, 10 or 15 of a month, like Ik', Eb and Kaban. [1] By name, those four are the four K'iche' year bearers: Iq', Kej, E and No'j. [3]

## The name: deer in the highlands, a puzzle in the lowlands

In the highlands the meaning is plain. Stuart notes that highland Mayan languages consistently use *keej* or a close cognate, the general word for "deer". [5] The Aztec day in this position is Mazatl, "deer", and the matching signs in Zapotec, Isthmian and other scripts show deer. [5][7] Kettunen and Helmke give Chij?, from *chij*, "deer", as a tentative Classic reading. [1]

The lowland names are harder. Stuart writes that "the Yukatek name Manik' is of obscure origin". A Ch'olan form, Manich', survives in baptismal records from Chiapas, where day names were used as personal names, and Tzeltalan languages had Moxik, also of unknown origin. [5] He agrees with linguist Terrence Kaufman that these names may originally have referred to a deer god or a deified patron of hunting, rather than simply "deer". [5]

In modern K'iche', Christenson's dictionary glosses *kej* as "horse". [4] His Popol Vuh notes still treat *kej* as "deer" when discussing the name of the hero Xbalanque. [8]

## Why is a deer day written with a hand?

J. Eric S. Thompson called Manik' "one of the most puzzling of all days". The name and the glyph, "a hand, shown sideways", seemed to have nothing to do with deer. [7] He proposed that the hand stood for the tail of a scorpion, which in Maya art sometimes ends in a hand, and linked the day to a god of hunting. [7]

Stuart's 2024 study answers part of the puzzle. Thompson did not know that Maya scribes "also occasionally employed a deer's head to represent Manik", and that "the hand and the deer head are interchangeable". [5] The same pair of signs is also used for the syllable *chi* and for the word *chij*, "deer". Stuart then proposes that the hand began as a deer's antler, a part standing for the whole, as the antler does for the day Deer at Cacaxtla and in Nahua writing. Over time scribes came to see it as a hand. [5] He is careful to add that "there is no known archaic form of the day Manik that displays an antler" and calls his proposal "a speculative extrapolation". [5]

## The earliest known Maya date

In 2022 Stuart and colleagues published painted mural fragments from San Bartolo, Guatemala, recording the day "7 Deer", that is, 7 Manik'. Sealed in an early phase of the Las Pinturas pyramid, they date to between 300 and 200 BCE, and the authors call it "the earliest securely dated example of the Maya calendar". [6] The day sign there is a deer's head, "its neck gracefully bent". [5]

On later Classic monuments, Manik' is a common day. Thompson counted 32 occurrences in his 1950 sample of non-period-ending dates, among the six most frequent. [7]

## Kej in highland tradition

The 260-day count is still kept in highland Guatemala by Ajq'ijab' (daykeepers). [2] Kej stands out in the records:

- **Year bearer.** Kej is one of the four days that begin and name a K'iche' year. [3] Christenson's dictionary calls it the "first ranked Mam (Year Bearer)". [4]
- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina calls Kej a "good day, on which beneficial things are asked for the supplicant". [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), call it "a good day above all others for requesting favors", tied to the ancestors; 8 Kej is the day of commemoration of the ancestors. [3]
- **Ixil Chajul, 1913.** Robert Burkitt recorded Txéh among the four "lucky days". [3]
- **Personal experience.** A woman from Santa Cruz del Quiché told Estrada Peña that on Kiej days she felt strong and agile, "because the corresponding animal was the deer". [12]

These records describe practice in particular communities. They are not forecasts for people born on Manik'. We are not daykeepers. Our reports use the deer's alertness and the hunter's patience as modern reflective imagery.

## Traditional Manik' vs Dreamspell "Blue Hand"

"Blue Hand" is seal 7 in the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990. [11] Dreamspell gives it the keywords Accomplishment, Knows and Healing. [10] It takes its name from the shape of the glyph, not from the deer that the day names in Maya languages.

The counts also differ: Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [11][13] 16 Oct 2026 is **7 Manik'** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 51, Blue Crystal Monkey. The next Dreamspell "Blue Hand" day, 12 Oct 2026 (Kin 47, Blue Galactic Hand), is **3 Ak'bal** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

- **Direction.** Prehispanic calendars assigned the days to east, north, west and south in turn, which places the seventh day in the **west**. [12] Contemporary K'iche' practice ties the west to Kame instead. [12]
- **Color.** Colonial Yucatec texts pair the west with black. [14] "Blue", shown on earlier versions of this site, is Dreamspell's color for this seal. [10]
- **Element.** Some charts give Manik' the element "air". We found no traditional source for Greek-style elements on day signs; on this site it appears only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Are Manik' and Kej the same day?**
Yes. Manik' (older spelling Manik) is the Yucatec name and Kej (Keej, Kiej) the K'iche' name for the seventh day. [1][3]

**Is Manik' the deer sign?**
Yes. The highland name means deer, the matching Mesoamerican days are "Deer", and Classic scribes sometimes wrote the day with a deer's head. [5][7]

**Why is the glyph a hand?**
The hand and deer head were interchangeable signs. Stuart proposes the hand began as a deer antler; he calls this speculative. [5]

**Is "Blue Hand" the same as Manik'?**
It fills the same slot in Dreamspell's list, but Dreamspell's count differs, so a "Blue Hand" birthday is usually not a Manik' day. [10][11]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [9][15]

**Is Manik' a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day that recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
