# Chuwen (B'atz'): The Monkey Day, Eleventh Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** Chuwen is the eleventh of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar; in K'iche' it is B'atz'. Both names point to the howler monkey, and Yucatec *aj chuen* means "artisan". In the Popol Vuh, the brothers One Batz and One Chouen, named after this day, were musicians, writers and sculptors. David Stuart traces the glyph to a portrait of the scribal monkey god. In highland Guatemala, 8 B'atz' marks the renewal of the 260-day cycle. Dreamspell calls this slot "Blue Monkey". [1][2][4][6][7]

## Where Chuwen sits in the 260-day count

Chuwen follows Ok and precedes Eb; 10 Ok is followed by 11 Chuwen. [1] In the 365-day Haab' it shares its possible positions (4, 9, 14 and 19 of a month) with Imix, Kimi and Kib. [1]

Its place in the count matters more in the highlands than elsewhere. K'iche' lists of the 20 days often begin with B'atz' rather than with Imox. [5] And daykeepers celebrate the Wajxaqib' B'atz' (8 B'atz') ceremony every 260 days to mark a new cycle, the Smithsonian notes. [2]

## What the names mean

Both names point to the same animal. Allen Christenson writes that *b'atz'* "is the Quiché word for the howler monkey", and that Chouen "is derived from the Yucatec Maya word *chuen*, meaning 'howler monkey'". [6] Christenson's dictionary lists *batz'*, "monkey", and the near-identical *bätz'*, "thread", along with words for spinning. [8] The resemblance between monkey and thread is a sound pattern in the dictionary; we have not found a source that links the two meanings for this day.

The Yucatec word also carries a craft meaning. Thompson quoted the colonial Motul dictionary: *ah chuen*, "artificer, craftsman of some art", and with other words "metal-smith", "potter" and "iron-smith". [4] Christenson notes that *aj chuen* is a title meaning "artisan", "consistent with the artistic nature" of the Popol Vuh twins. [6] The Aztec day in this position was Ozomatli, "monkey", and Sahagún reported that children born on it were expected to become singers, dancers, painters or craftsmen. [4]

## One Batz and One Chouen in the Popol Vuh

The Popol Vuh tells of two brothers, One Batz and One Chouen, sons of One Hunahpu and half-brothers of the hero twins Hunahpu and Xbalanque. Christenson's translation says they "became flautists and singers, writers and sculptors, jade workers and precious metalsmiths". [6] Jealous of their younger brothers, they were tricked into climbing a tree and turned into monkeys. [4] Christenson adds that "twin monkey scribes appear frequently in Classic Maya art as patrons of writing". [6]

The brothers' names are calendar names. Like many K'iche' people in colonial times, they carry the name of a day in the 260-day count. [3][4]

## The glyph

Thompson found the symbolic form of the glyph "too stylized to yield any meaning", though the head variants "probably represent monkeys". [4] Stuart now explains why. He writes that "the day sign for Chuen was first a portrait of the scribal monkey deity, and its eye came to be used as its standard shorthand representation". [7] Over time, he argues, complex portraits of gods were simplified "through expediency and convention" into the abstract shapes we see in the codices. [7] The same process turned the Ix jaguar into an eye and the Imix serpent into a flower.

## B'atz' in highland tradition

The 260-day count is still kept in highland Guatemala by Ajq'ijab' (daykeepers). [2] The records show two faces of B'atz':

- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina's calendar lists B'atz' as a "bad day" on which illness was prayed upon enemies. [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), describe it instead as a "good day, symbolic of continuity with the past or ancestors". [3]
- **Craft and learning.** Thompson noted that in Schultze Jena's K'iche' list it is a good day for prayers about household furnishings, and a Chichicastenango informant called it good for learning. [4]
- **New cycle.** 8 B'atz' (Wajxaqib' B'atz') is the occasion for the ceremony that opens a new 260-day cycle. [2]

These records describe community practice. They are not forecasts for anyone born on Chuwen. We are not daykeepers. Our reports draw on the image of the artisan, the scribe and the thread as modern reflective writing.

## Traditional Chuwen vs Dreamspell "Blue Monkey"

"Blue Monkey" is seal 11 in the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990. [11] Dreamspell gives it the keywords Magic, Plays and Illusion. [10] The animal matches the Maya day, but those keywords are Dreamspell's, not the artisan and scribe themes of Maya sources.

The counts also differ: Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [11][12] 20 Oct 2026 is **11 Chuwen** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 55, Blue Electric Eagle. The next Dreamspell "Blue Monkey" day, 16 Oct 2026 (Kin 51, Blue Crystal Monkey), is **7 Manik'** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

- **Direction.** Prehispanic calendars assigned the days to east, north, west and south in turn, which places the eleventh day in the **west**. [5] Contemporary K'iche' practice ties the west to Kame instead. [5]
- **Color.** Colonial Yucatec texts pair the west with black. [13] "Blue", shown on earlier versions of this site, is Dreamspell's color for this seal. [10]
- **Element.** Some charts give Chuwen the element "air". We found no traditional source for Greek-style elements on day signs; on this site it appears only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Are Chuwen, Chuen and B'atz' the same day?**
Yes. Chuen is the colonial Yucatec spelling, Chuwen the revised one, and B'atz' the K'iche' name. [1][3]

**Is Chuwen the monkey sign?**
Yes. Both *chuen* and *b'atz'* name the howler monkey, and the glyph began as a portrait of the monkey scribe god. [6][7]

**What is Wajxaqib' B'atz'?**
The day 8 B'atz', on which K'iche' daykeepers hold the ceremony that opens a new 260-day cycle. [2]

**Is "Blue Monkey" the same as Chuwen?**
It fills the same slot in Dreamspell's list, but Dreamspell's count differs, so a "Blue Monkey" birthday is usually not a Chuwen day. [10][11]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [9][14]

**Is Chuwen a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day that recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
