# Etz'nab (Tijax): The Flint Day, Eighteenth Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** Etz'nab is the eighteenth of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar; in K'iche' it is Tijax. Its glyph shows a pressure-flaked blade of flint or obsidian, and the central Mexican day in the same position is "flint knife". Colonial Yucatec lists associate it with the healer who bleeds a fever; in the K'iche' highlands it is a day for confessing quarrels. "Mirror", common online, is the Dreamspell name, not a traditional meaning. [1][3][5]

## Where Etz'nab sits in the 260-day count

Etz'nab follows Kaban and precedes Kawak; 4 Kaban is followed by 5 Etz'nab. [1] In the 365-day Haab' Etz'nab can only fall on positions 1, 6, 11 or 16 of a month, the same set as Ak'bal, Lamat and Ben. [1]

## The glyph: a flaked blade

Of all the day signs, Etz'nab may be the easiest to recognize. Thompson wrote in 1950 that "the standard glyph of Etz'nab has been identified as a picture of a pressure-flaked blade", the same design often drawn on stone or obsidian spearheads, and that in early examples "the resemblance is much more apparent". [5] Pressure flaking is the technique used to finish the finest blades of flint and obsidian.

The Yucatec name fits the picture. Thompson cites the Yucatec scholar Alfredo Barrera Vásquez, who showed that Etz'nab "is a sharpened implement", with words in the colonial Motul dictionary that suggest pressure-flaking. [5]

## The highland names: Tijax and Chinax

The K'iche' name is Tijax (Tihax in all three colonial calendars edited by Weeks, Sachse and Prager), and in Chiapas the day was Chinax. [3][5] Thompson suggested that both contain a word for "mouth", noting that flint and obsidian blades in Mexican codices are often drawn "with a mouth and a formidable set of teeth", as if the knife bit into flesh. He cites Schultze Jena's link between Tijax and a K'iche' root meaning "to bite", and the friar Ximénez's gloss "death cutting asunder", "obsidian knife". [5] Christenson's dictionary lists Tijax as a day in the sacred calendar. [6]

## Flint in the ancient texts

**A healer's blade.** In the colonial Kaua manuscript the augury for Etz'nab reads, in Barrera Vásquez's translation: "The bleeder for fevers. The sharpened flint. The mot-mot bird is its augury. In good health. Medical bleeder, medical healer also. Valiant also." Thompson added that bleeding for fever with a chip of flint or glass was still practiced among the Maya of his time. [5]

**A myth at Palenque.** The hieroglyphic platform of Temple XIX at Palenque, dedicated in 734 CE, tells how the god GI decapitated a crocodile in mythical time "on a day 1 Eznab" (1 Etz'nab), and how its spilled blood caused a flood. The anthropologist Canek Estrada Peña summarizes this from the epigraphers David Stuart and Erik Velásquez. [4] The crocodile ties the story to Imix, the first day sign.

**A god of sacrifice?** Thompson proposed that Etz'nab was the day of "some god connected with the flint or obsidian knife", possibly a deity of sacrifice. [5] This was a guess, and he said so ("perhaps"); we report it only as his hypothesis.

## Tijax in highland tradition

The 260-day count is still kept in highland Guatemala by Ajq'ijab' (daykeepers). [2] Tijax is linked to conflict and to repairing it:

- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina's calendar calls Tijax a "good day, the same as the preceding". [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), describe it as "the day of quarrels and evil words. The day 8 Tijax is a good day to confess sins, especially quarrels with one's wife, relatives, or parents." [3]

The pairing is worth noticing: the day that names quarrels is also the day set aside for settling them. These records describe practice, not the temperament of people born on Etz'nab. We are not daykeepers. Our reports use the image of the blade, which cuts and also heals, as modern reflective writing about honesty and repair.

## Why "Mirror" is not a traditional meaning

Many websites, including earlier versions of this one, label Etz'nab "Mirror". That name comes from the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990, where this slot is seal 18, "White Mirror", with the keywords Endlessness, Reflects and Order. [8][9] Maya and central Mexican sources point consistently to flint and obsidian. [5] Polished obsidian was used for mirrors in Mesoamerica, which may explain the modern choice, but we found no traditional source that names the day "mirror".

The counts also differ: Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [9][10] 27 Oct 2026 is **5 Etz'nab** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 62, White Planetary Wind. The next Dreamspell "White Mirror" day, 23 Oct 2026 (Kin 58, White Rhythmic Mirror), is **1 Ix** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

- **Direction.** Prehispanic calendars assigned the days to east, north, west and south in turn, which places the eighteenth day in the **north**. [11] Contemporary K'iche' practice gives the north to Imox instead. [11]
- **Color.** The north is white in colonial Yucatec texts; assigning that color to one day is a modern reconstruction. [12] Dreamspell also colors this seal white. [8]
- **Element.** Some charts, including earlier versions of this site, give Etz'nab the element "air". We found no traditional source for Greek-style elements on day signs; on this site it appears only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Are Etz'nab and Tijax the same day?**
Yes. Etz'nab is the Yucatec name and Tijax the K'iche' name for the eighteenth day. [1][3]

**Does Etz'nab mean "mirror"?**
No. "White Mirror" is the Dreamspell name. Traditional sources describe a flint or obsidian blade. [5][8]

**What does Etz'nab mean?**
"A sharpened implement", according to Barrera Vásquez; the glyph shows a flaked blade. [5]

**Is "White Mirror" the same as Etz'nab?**
It fills the same slot in Dreamspell's list, but Dreamspell's count differs, so a "White Mirror" birthday is usually not an Etz'nab day. [8][9]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [7][13]

**Is Etz'nab a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day that recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
