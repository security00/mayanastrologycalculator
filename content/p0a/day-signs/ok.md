# Ok (Tz'i'): The Dog Day, Tenth Sign of the Maya Calendar

*Prepared by the Mayan Astrology Calculator editors · Sources below · Reviewed 10 October 2026 · Calculation: gmt584283-proleptic-gregorian-v1*

> **Quick answer.** Ok is the tenth of the 20 day signs in the 260-day Maya calendar; in K'iche' it is Tz'i', the ordinary word for "dog". The glyph is the head of an animal, probably a dog, and across Mesoamerica the matching day is "Dog". In Maya and Mexican belief, dogs guided the dead to the underworld, and J. Eric S. Thompson linked the Yucatec name to *ocol*, "to enter", used of the setting sun. Dreamspell calls this slot "White Dog". [1][3][4][5]

## Where Ok sits in the 260-day count

Ok follows Muluk and precedes Chuwen; 9 Muluk is followed by 10 Ok. [1] It is the midpoint of the 20 names, and over 260 days it meets every number once. [2] In the 365-day Haab' it can only fall on positions 3, 8, 13 or 18 of a month, the same set as Chikchan, Men and Ajaw. [1]

## The names: Ok, Tz'i' and Elab

The highland name is transparent. Christenson's K'iche' dictionary lists Tz'i' as the day and *tz'i'* as "dog". [5] Thompson observed that *tz'i* or *chi* is the usual word for dog "in all other Maya languages and dialects" except a few, while Yucatec uses quite different words (*pek*, among others). [4] That makes the Yucatec name Ok the odd one out.

Thompson offered two leads. A Ch'ol word list gives *ok* for dog in Palenque Ch'ol. And Yucatec *oc* is the root of *ocol*, "to enter", used for sunset (*ocol kin*), when the sun entered the earth. [4] Kaua divinatory lists contain the phrase *Ah ocencab*, which Thompson read as "he who descends, and enters the earth", "precisely the role of the dog". [4] A third name, Elab, used in Chiapas and by the Chuj, may come from a Yucatec root for burning, which Thompson connected with stories of the dog bringing fire to people. [4] He presented all three as possibilities, not proof.

Kettunen and Helmke give Ook? as a tentative Classic reading. [1]

## The glyph and the dog of the underworld

The day sign is the head of an animal "which may well be that of a dog". [4] In the codices, Thompson noted, the ear alone often replaces the whole head, and the darkness sign set above a dog's eye suggests a connection with the underworld. [4]

That connection is documented in several places. In Central Mexican belief, a dog sacrificed at its master's death guided the soul to Mictlan, the land of the dead, helping it across a wide stretch of water. Thompson gathered Maya parallels: the Lacandon placed small palm-leaf dogs at the corners of a grave to accompany the soul; dog remains were found in tombs at Kaminaljuyu; the Tzeltal of Tenejapa said dogs should be treated well because they lead the souls of the dead; people at Chenalhó believed dogs help their masters cross the river of the dead. [4] The Central Mexican dog god Xolotl, who guided the sun into the underworld at evening, was in Thompson's view the counterpart of the Maya patron of this day, whose own name is unknown. [4]

Thompson also noted that a dog's head, marked with crossed bones, sometimes replaces the usual "day" sign in the Lunar Series, which he read as the night sun. [4] That is his interpretation from 1950.

## Tz'i' in highland tradition

The 260-day count is still kept in highland Guatemala by Ajq'ijab' (daykeepers). [2] The highland records of Tz'i' are among the least favorable:

- **1854 manuscript.** Vicente Hernández Spina's calendar lists Tz'i' as a "bad day". [3]
- **20th-century fieldwork.** Weeks, Sachse and Prager, summarizing Bunzel (1952) and Tedlock (1992), say Tz'i' "symbolizes sin", and that there were no ceremonies on the day. [3]
- **A sequence of days.** Bunzel's collaborator, quoted in Christenson's Popol Vuh notes, read 7 Q'anil, 8 Toj, 9 Tz'i' together as a sequence; in it, "ts'i', 'dog'" stands for "some shameless act". [6]
- **Other senses of the word.** Christenson's dictionary also lists the adjective *tz'i'*, "foolish; disobedient (child)". [5]

These are records of how some communities read the day. They are not statements about people born on Ok. We are not daykeepers. Our reports draw on the dog as companion and guide, the one who knows the way through the dark, as modern reflective writing.

## Traditional Ok vs Dreamspell "White Dog"

"White Dog" is seal 10 in the **Dreamspell**, a modern system begun by José and Lloydine Argüelles in 1987 and published as a kit in 1990. [10] Dreamspell gives it the keywords Heart, Loves and Loyalty. [9] The animal matches the Maya day; the keywords are Dreamspell's.

The counts differ. Dreamspell fixes 26 July 1987 as "Kin 34, White Galactic Wizard" and skips 29 February. [10][11] 19 Oct 2026 is **10 Ok** in the traditional count; Dreamspell calls it Kin 54, White Lunar Wizard. The next Dreamspell "White Dog" day, 15 Oct 2026 (Kin 50, White Spectral Dog), is **6 Kimi** in the traditional count. ## Colors, directions and "elements"

- **Direction.** Prehispanic calendars assigned the days to east, north, west and south in turn, which places the tenth day in the **north**. [7] Contemporary K'iche' practice gives the north to Imox instead. [7]
- **Color.** "White" follows the north–white pairing of colonial Yucatec texts; assigning it to one day is a modern reconstruction. [12] Dreamspell also colors this seal white. [9]
- **Element.** Some charts, including earlier versions of this site, give Ok the element "earth". We found no traditional source for Greek-style elements on day signs; on this site it appears only as a labeled modern convention.

## FAQ

**Are Ok, Oc and Tz'i' the same day?**
Yes. Oc is the colonial Yucatec spelling, Ok the revised one, and Tz'i' the K'iche' name. [1][3]

**Is Ok the dog sign?**
Yes. Tz'i' means dog in K'iche', the glyph is probably a dog's head, and the matching Aztec day is Itzcuintli, "dog". [4][5]

**Why is the dog linked to death?**
In Maya and Central Mexican belief, dogs guided the souls of the dead to the underworld. [4]

**Is "White Dog" the same as Ok?**
It fills the same slot in Dreamspell's list, but Dreamspell's count differs, so a "White Dog" birthday is usually not an Ok day. [9][10]

**Why does another calculator give me a different day sign?**
It may use the Dreamspell count, or a correlation constant other than 584283, which shifts results by a day or two. [8][13]

**Is Ok a zodiac sign?**
No. It names a day that recurs every 20 days. → [How we calculate](/methodology)
